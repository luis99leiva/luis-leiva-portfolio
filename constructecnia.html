Constructecnia

The website for Paraguay’s largest construction trade fair, designed and built for its 2024 edition.

At Grupo Paraguay Eventos & Emprendimientos I designed and developed the website for Constructecnia 2024, a five-day fair with 25,000 attendees, and managed it alongside the company’s own site.

Role
UX/UI DesignerDesign and development

Grupo Paraguay EventosAsunción, hybrid

Scope
Event websiteLive streams, gallery, live updates
01The brief
Two audiences

One site for visitors and exhibitors

The fair needed a site that worked for two groups at once, companies deciding whether to exhibit, and visitors who just wanted to know when, where and what was on. The home page answers both above the fold, with the dates and venue, the confirmed exhibitors, and two clear paths, to become an exhibitor or to attend for free.

25,000

Attendees at the 2024 fair

5

Days of fair, May 22 to 26

2

Sites I managed, this one and the company’s

02What I built
Plan your visit

Find a stand, find a talk

A trade fair is a big place to walk. The floor plan lets visitors filter by pavilion (industry, housing, machinery, international) and links each stand to an exhibitor card. The activities page lists every workshop and talk day by day, with the time, the room and who is speaking.

03During the fair
Live content

A site that kept up with the event

Once the doors opened, the site had to change as fast as the fair did. I built live-streaming pages, a photo gallery for moments like the painters’ contest, and a feed of the latest Instagram and Facebook posts, so the home page always showed what was happening that day.

04Data
With marketing

Let the numbers pick the next change

I worked with the marketing team and used Google Analytics to decide what to change on the site, with one goal in mind, keeping the audience engaged before and during the fair.

05Reflection
What I took from it

Event sites live on a deadline

Everything builds toward five days, and then the content changes every hour. It taught me to design pages that are quick to update while the event is running. Both sites have since been redesigned; the screens here are the version I designed and launched.
